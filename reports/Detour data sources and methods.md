# Kazakh statistics and dated events sharpen detour tracking

Researchers track CHPL goods from the EU and China through Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan to Russia with the same core tool as our project: partner-reported HS6 trade, compared across the two legs of the route. They add four things that our project does not have yet: control goods and control countries (EBRD), Russian transaction-level customs records (KSE, Verstka, iStories, The Insider), weapon teardowns that tell which codes matter (CAR, HUR), and dated enforcement events. The most useful new data source for our project is **Kazakhstan's Bureau of National Statistics (BNS)**. It publishes monthly, cumulative files of trade with EAEU partners, including Russia, at 10-digit TN VED, with a lag of about 45–50 days. Its latest file covers **January–September 2026**. This file can fill our gap in Kazakhstan's monthly Comtrade data (March–December 2024) and extend the series into 2026. For methods, the best value per effort is a dated event timeline, a unit-value check, a "neighbour code" check around the April 2026 ban, and a simple difference-in-differences with control codes and control countries. Our main findings agree with the published record in direction: China dominates supply, Kazakhstan peaked early and Kyrgyzstan is the persistent problem. Three of our results appear to be new: the Kyrgyz mirror ratio by year, the mid-2023 start of the fall in monthly data, and the post-ban effect on the two banned codes (EU −92%, China +71% to Kyrgyzstan). We found no other public work that measures these.

## Kazakhstan's own statistics office beats Comtrade by two years

The BNS table page "Export and import with EAEU countries by 4, 6, 10 digit TN VED" lists xlsx files for **January–September 2026 (released 16 November 2026)**, January–August 2026 (released 15 October 2026), and full years 2021–2025 ([BNS spreadsheets](https://stat.gov.kz/ru/industries/economy/foreign-market/spreadsheets/?name=40111)). The same section also has "commodity-by-country" and "country-by-commodity" files for all partners, trade by oblast, and high-tech exports by region (same source; [related table](https://stat.gov.kz/ru/industries/economy/foreign-market/spreadsheets/?name=40108)). BNS compiles mutual EAEU trade under the EAEU method for mutual-trade statistics, from statistical forms, not from customs declarations ([stat.gov.kz methodology](https://stat.gov.kz/api/iblock/element/451974/file/en/)). By contrast, KSE's Q1 2026 tracker says that Kazakhstan's Comtrade data end at **February 2024** ([KSE CHP tracker Q1 2026](https://sanctions.kse.ua/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/KSE_CHP_tracker_Q1_2026-1.pdf)). That agrees with our own gap from March 2024. Bruegel uses Kazakhstan as a mirror reporter, and its Kazakhstan series runs to November 2025 ([Bruegel tracker](https://www.bruegel.org/dataset/russian-foreign-trade-tracker)).

Three points about the BNS files are not known yet. The page does not say whether the files have weight or quantity columns. We do not know whether BNS suppresses CHPL lines to Russia as confidential. The files are cumulative year-to-date, so monthly values need differencing, and revisions between releases can give negative months. The notes did not download the file. A download needs the user's approval (Jan–Sep 2026 file: xlsx, about 9.8 MB, from stat.gov.kz).

The other two countries give less. Kyrgyzstan's National Statistical Committee publishes monthly trade by TN VED, but its customs-based partner detail covers only countries outside the EAEU ([stat.gov.kg methodology 216](https://stat.gov.kg/ru/statistics/download/methodology/216)). Kyrgyz trade with Russia comes from statistical forms under another method ([economist.kg](https://economist.kg/ekonomika/2026/07/20/torgovlya-kyrgyzstana-vne-eaes.md)). Kyrgyz media cite a customs open-data portal with weights by partner and part-month data ([economist.kg, Oct 2026](https://economist.kg/ekonomika/2026/10/16/import-eksport-po-vesu-vne-eaes.md)), but its host did not resolve during the research. This portal can be useful for the China→Kyrgyzstan leg, not for Kyrgyzstan→Russia. Uzbekistan's SIAT portal has an open SDMX API under CC BY 4.0, but the monthly export dataset is 2-digit only and has no partner split ([SIAT 3079](https://siat.stat.uz/data/3079/?lang=en)). For Uzbekistan, Comtrade stays the main HS6 × partner source.

Several other sources fail or add nothing. The Eurasian Economic Commission's detailed external and mutual trade tables stop at **January 2022** ([EEC tables](https://eec.eaeunion.org/comission/department/dep_stat/tradestat/tables/)). Treat them as discontinued. Russia stopped product-level customs publication in April 2022 and has no plans to resume ([Barents Observer](https://thebarentsobserver.com/en/node/9679)). TrendEconomy and WITS republish Comtrade data, so they add nothing to our keyed Comtrade access ([WITS](https://wits.worldbank.org/trade-data/monthlydata/en/country/KAZ/year/LTST/monthly)).

China's customs agency (GACC) is the primary source for China's HS8 × partner monthly data. KSE already uses it for months that are not yet in Comtrade. KSE says GACC figures are "very tightly aligned" with later Comtrade data, and it drops the latest two months as incomplete ([KSE CHP tracker Q1 2026](https://sanctions.kse.ua/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/KSE_CHP_tracker_Q1_2026-1.pdf)). Automated access is fragile. A fetch of stats.customs.gov.cn failed with a certificate error during the research, and the access rules for 2026 (registration, captcha, query caps) are not confirmed. CEIC sells GACC HS8 monthly series ([CEIC](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/china/rmb-hs90-optical-photographic-cinematographic-measuring-checking-precision-medical-or-surgical-instruments-and-apparatus-parts-and-accessories-thereof/cn-export-hs-8-manostats)). US Census and TurkStat monthly HS data by partner can give US and Türkiye first legs, but the notes did not verify their detail or lag [U].

## Teardowns choose the codes; events and controls test the routes

The literature uses aggregate HS6 data with controls as its workhorse. The EBRD design compares sanctioned and non-sanctioned codes, and target and control countries, before and after 2022. The journal version (European Economic Review, 2026) finds that transshipment plus "phantom transit" offset **about one third** of the fall in European exports to Russia in 2022–23 ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/eecrev/v187y2026ics001429212600084x.html)). Its earlier version used Mongolia and Azerbaijan as comparators ([CEPR DP20097](https://cepr.org/publications/DP20097)). Our project can copy a simple version of this design with open data.

Transaction-level Russian import records are the strongest evidence, because they show brand, consignee, country of origin and "country of dispatch". The dispatch-versus-origin split shows the Kazakh and Kyrgyz role that HS6 data hide. iStories used declarations to show "false transit": the declared sender was China, Kazakhstan or Azerbaijan, but the chips entered through the EU border ([Euromaidan Press on iStories](https://euromaidanpress.com/2024/10/18/media-investigation-uncovers-russias-potential-sanctions-evasion-through-transit-manipulation/)). But this source is closing. The Insider reports that about **180 high-tech categories stopped appearing in the data at the end of 2024** and show zero in 2025 ([The Insider](https://theins.press/en/inv/288081)). KSE says unofficial access declined after 2023 ([KSE CHP tracker Q1 2026](https://sanctions.kse.ua/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/KSE_CHP_tracker_Q1_2026-1.pdf)). For our project, this is a "cite the findings" source. Do not republish raw leaked records, because of legal risk and personal names.

Weapon teardowns tell which codes matter, but not which route. The IISS/CAR paper (September 2025) finds that **under 10% of CAR's component sample is outside the CHPL**. More than 25% of Shahed-136-pattern components and about 40% of Kh-69 components are CHPL Tier 1. CAR also finds strong localisation: 29% of components with a 2021 date mark were Russian, against 90–94% for 2023–2024 marks, for the part types it studied ([IISS/CAR](https://www.iiss.org/globalassets/media-library---content--migration/files/research-papers/2025/09/pub25-094-tracking-the-components-of-missiles-and-uavs-used-by-russia-in-ukraine.pdf)). CAR itself warns that it "cannot definitively attribute all components to a single HS customs code" (same source). Ukraine's HUR War&Sanctions portal lists more than 5,200 foreign components in 181 weapon types, per secondary reports ([Espreso](https://global.espreso.tv/russia-ukraine-war-ukrainian-intelligence-reveals-over-100-foreign-components-in-russian-drones-and-missiles)). Use these sources to weight or rank CHPL codes on the site, not to measure flows.

Designation lists are free, machine-readable and dated. BIS added a Kazakh and a Kyrgyz entity in August 2024 ([Federal Register, 27 Aug 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-27/html/2024-19130.htm)). The US sanctioned Uzbek firms in October 2024 ([Daryo](https://daryo.uz/en/2024/10/31/u-s-imposes-sanctions-on-uzbek-companies-over-links-to-russian-defense-industry-1201656/)). KSE uses OpenSanctions entries as references ([KSE July 2025](https://institute.kse.ua/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/disassembling_the_russian_war_machine_logistics_chokepoints_and_dependencies_eng_july_2025.pdf)). A count of organisation designations by country and month gives a second event series to plot against trade.

Price and code-switching tests are possible with the quantity fields in Comext and Comtrade. Chupilkin, Javorcik and Peeva find that unit values of Western-trademark goods that reach Russia rose up to 35 percentage points more than those of neutral brands ([AEA 2025](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2025/program/paper/bYHQEFA9)). EBRD found only small mark-ups at the point of export and "limited (if any)" misclassification ([Plekhanov, BOFIT 2023](https://www.suomenpankki.fi/globalassets/bofit/research/conferences-and-workshops/2023/session-1---alexander-plekhanov.pdf)). Atradius calls misclassification into adjacent codes a blind spot of standard trade data ([Atradius](https://atradius.co.uk/reports/economic-research-are-sanctions-on-strategic-exports-to-russia-effective.html)). The April 2026 ban gives a clean test: if EU exports to Kyrgyzstan of sibling HS6 codes in HS4 8457 and 8517 rise while 845710 and 851762 fall, that is a reclassification signal. No study has done this test for Central Asia.

Logistics data give context, not detection. KSE used Russian rail cargo records and found that machinery and component shipments entering from Kazakhstan rose from **2 kt (2021) to 59 kt (2022) and 179 kt (2023), and stayed stable in 2024** ([KSE July 2025](https://institute.kse.ua/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/disassembling_the_russian_war_machine_logistics_chokepoints_and_dependencies_eng_july_2025.pdf)). The rail data are not public. This result is important for our project. Our Kazakh-reported CHPL value fell after 2022, but KSE's physical tonnage via Kazakhstan tripled in 2023. The goods groups are different (all machinery and components against 49 CHPL codes), so the comparison is not exact. But one possible reading is that recorded CHPL value fell while physical flows did not. That reading is a hypothesis.

Machine-learning anomaly detection on Comtrade price-per-kg exists only in preprints on other goods ([arXiv 2512.07864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07864?context=cs)). No published synthetic-control or event-study paper on Central Asian re-exports to Russia was found. This is a gap that our event study can fill.

No 2024–2026 work in the notes publishes monthly EU and China→KZ/KG/UZ and KZ/KG/UZ→Russia series at CHPL HS6 level, with mirror gaps, as open data. EBRD stops at 2023. KSE's quarterly tracker looks at Russia's import side and pools Central Asia. NGO reports give headline percentages without files. The table compares our results with the closest external figures.

Two disputes in the literature matter for how our site words its claims. The first is about scale. EBRD finds that detours offset about one third of lost EU exports in 2022–23 ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/eecrev/v187y2026ics001429212600084x.html)). KSE "casts doubt" on claims that flows simply moved to Central Asia, because China now supplies Russia directly ([KSE](https://sanctions.kse.ua/en/export-controls-have-reduced-but-not-eliminated-russia-s-access-to-war-critical-technology)). Both can be true. Central Asia matters for EU-origin goods but is small in Russia's total supply. Euromaidan Press attributes to KSE a Kyrgyz share of **1.1%** of Russia's battlefield-goods imports in 2023 ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/03/19/china-sells-62-of-russias-weapons-making-machines-eu-sanctions-target-kyrgyzstan/)). Our China-to-EU ratio of 3.5 supports the KSE side for the first leg.

The second dispute is about under-reporting. KSE's Q3 2026 tracker adds transaction data and finds that recorded volumes for Tiers 1, 2 and 3.A are **3–4 times lower** than transaction volumes. Most hidden volume is in China (about USD 6–7 bn) and Hong Kong (about USD 5–6 bn), and coalition countries account for less than USD 1 bn ([KSE Q3 2026](https://institute.kse.ua/news/official-trade-statistics-may-not-reflect-the-full-volume-of-imports-of-military-goods-into-russia-russian-chp-imports-tracker-for-the-third-quarter-of-2026/)). This supports a mirror check of the China→Central Asia leg, not only the EU leg. It also means our second leg is a lower bound.

Our project also lacks some things that others have: econometric controls (EBRD), Russia-wide coverage and Hong Kong (KSE), unit counts for CNC machines (ESCU says China supplied 62% of USD 18.2 bn of CNC imports in January 2023–July 2024, method not published ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/03/19/china-sells-62-of-russias-weapons-making-machines-eu-sanctions-target-kyrgyzstan/))), and firm and bank names (journalists). For Uzbekistan, the notes found no 2024–2026 quantitative study. The EU reported "certain successes" in cutting military-use exports via Uzbekistan in May 2025 ([Kun.uz](https://kun.uz/en/news/2025/05/28/eu-reports-success-in-blocking-military-use-exports-to-russia-via-uzbekistan-and-others)). Uzbekistan is not in the EAEU, so its exports to Russia go through customs declarations. They are probably better recorded than intra-EAEU flows (inference, not verified).

The mid-2023 timing gives a clear test of competing explanations. Kazakhstan's fall from January 2023 comes before almost all of these events. It lines up best with the Western outreach of early 2023 and the Kazakh screening announcement of March 2023. Kyrgyzstan's fall from July 2023 lines up with the EU 11th package of 23 June 2023, US pressure in June 2023, and possible US designations of Kyrgyz entities in July 2023 [U]. No study confirms either link. Both are hypotheses. Note also a non-policy explanation: CAR's date marks show rapid localisation of components in Russia from 2023 ([IISS/CAR](https://www.iiss.org/globalassets/media-library---content--migration/files/research-papers/2025/09/pub25-094-tracking-the-components-of-missiles-and-uavs-used-by-russia-in-ukraine.pdf)), and China's direct supply to Russia grew. Demand for the detour can fall without any enforcement event.

The April 2026 ban is the cleanest experiment, because it names one country, two codes and clear dates. The control groups come from our own data: other CHPL codes to Kyrgyzstan, and the same two codes to Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan. The end of grandfathered contracts on **25 July 2026** gives a second break date. The EU Commission based the ban on its own figures (+800% EU→KG, +1,200% KG→RU). The notes do not confirm the exact base period or the code list of these figures. Our mirror ratio and CN8 detail can test that evidence independently.

## Conclusion

The research changes the project's best position. Our first draft of the evidence relied on Comtrade, which leaves Kazakhstan blind after February 2024. Kazakhstan's own statistics office removes that blind spot, so the project can follow the main corridor almost in real time, which no public tracker does. The literature also shows that the detour story is now less about volume and more about timing and response. Russia gets most CHPL goods directly from China, and Central Asia is a small share. But Central Asia is where policy is tested: the EU used its anti-circumvention tool there first, and our data show both the effect (EU −92%) and the leak (China +71%) within months. That substitution is the most policy-relevant result in the project, and nobody else has published it.

Two cautions follow. First, every recorded figure on the second leg is a lower bound, because transaction data show 3–4 times more than official statistics for key tiers. Second, the fall from mid-2023 started before most enforcement events, so the site must not suggest that any single event caused it. The strongest claim the project can make is comparative: what changed for the banned codes, against controls, after a dated event. Steps 1–4 deliver that claim with open data in about a week.
