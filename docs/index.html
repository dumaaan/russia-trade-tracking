Battlefield goods via Central Asia

After February 2022, EU exports of Common High Priority List (CHPL) goods to Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan went up sharply. At the same time, both countries reported much higher exports of the same goods to Russia. This page shows both flows and compares the two countries. The exports to Russia are a lower bound: trade inside the Eurasian Economic Union has no customs declarations and is under-reported.

    Kazakhstan versus Kyrgyzstan

    Kazakhstan's flows are much larger. Kyrgyzstan's grew much faster from a small base.

    Read the ratio with care. The EU is not the only supplier. China and other countries also sell these goods to Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, so the exports to Russia can be larger than the EU exports. For example, Kazakhstan's Tier 3.B exports to Russia (bearings, optics) are larger than the EU's Tier 3.B exports to Kazakhstan in every year.

    Mirror check: does the EU export arrive?

    What Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan record as imports from the EU-27, divided by what the EU records as exports to them. Below 100%, part of the EU exports is not in the country's import records.

    A gap is normal before the war too: goods can arrive in another year, importers record the country of origin (the EU records the destination), and imports include freight and insurance (CIF). So compare each year with the country's own pre-war ratio. The excess gap is the part of the gap above that pre-war level. Kazakhstan gives no product detail for imports by partner for 2020–2022, so its pre-war ratio uses 2019 only.

    Method and limits

    • CHPL list. EU version of February 2024: 50 HS codes in Tiers 1–4 (integrated circuits, communication parts, bearings, optics, CNC machine tools and others). Earlier versions had 9 codes (May 2023) and 45 codes (September 2023), so totals from 2023 studies are not directly comparable.
    • Basket of 49 codes. Code 854149 is not in the totals: its HS 2017 code (854140) also has solar cells and LEDs, so the 2019–2021 values would be much too high. Its data stays in the code-level file.
    • EU exports. Eurostat Comext, dataset DS-045409, reporter EU-27, HS6 level, in EUR. The USD values use the ECB annual average rate. The EUR view uses the same rate, so the Comext values are the original ones and the Comtrade values are converted.
    • Mirror check. UN Comtrade imports reported by Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan from each of the 27 EU states, summed. Importers record the country of origin and CIF values; the EU records the destination and FOB values.
    • Exports to Russia. UN Comtrade, as reported by Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan. Russia does not publish product-level trade data after 2021.
    • HS versions. Data for 2019–2021 uses HS 2017. Codes 880730 and 852589 use their HS 2017 codes (880330, 852580) in those years.
    • Baseline. The main baseline is the 2019–2021 average, because one year can be unusual and small values make a one-year base unstable. 2021 alone is a second option.
    • Missing data. When a source has no data for a year, the value is missing, not zero. When a source has data for a year, a code with no row is zero trade.
    • Limits. Exports to Russia are probably under-reported (see the cross-checks). An HS6 code is wider than the sanctioned goods. Two rising lines can show re-export, but they do not prove it: the EU leg also has normal post-2022 import growth.
