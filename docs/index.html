The Central Asian detour

Battlefield goods are the 50 types (customs codes) of electronics, bearings and machine tools that Western governments found in Russian weapons in Ukraine. The map follows them from the EU and from China through Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan into Russia. The site uses 49 of the 50 codes: the code for light sensors has no comparable data before 2022.

    Part 2 How good the records are

    Part of the EU's shipments never appear in the importers' records

    Each line divides what the country records as imports from the EU by what the EU records as exports to it. Below 100%, part of the shipments is missing from the country's records.

    Some gap is normal: goods arrive in another year, importers record the country of origin, and import values include freight. So each year is compared with the country's own pre-war ratio. The excess gap is the part above that level. Kazakhstan gives no product detail for imports by partner for 2020–2022, so its pre-war ratio uses 2019 only.

    Show the numbers

    The same check for China

    Imports from China as each country records them, divided by China's exports to it as China records them. Kazakhstan gives no product detail for imports from China for 2020–2022. Importers record the country of origin, so Chinese goods re-exported by Hong Kong count as imports from China: this can put a ratio above 100%.

    Listed goods as a share of all trade

    Each line tracks listed battlefield goods as a share of total trade value—on the left, EU exports to the country; on the right, the country's exports to Russia.

    A flat line means these goods are keeping pace with overall trade. A rising line means they are outpacing it. All eight charts share one scale.

    Comparison countries

    Did listed goods grow faster than similar goods?

    Each chart compares the listed goods with the other goods of the same customs chapters: machines (84), electronics (85) and instruments (90). The lines are times the pre-war level, on one log scale for all charts. Where the coloured lines are above the grey line, the listed goods grew faster.

    Many of the other goods are also under EU export bans to Russia, so this compares priority goods with other technical goods, not banned goods with free goods. Bearings (8482) are most of Kazakhstan's pre-war listed exports to Russia, so the results are given with and without them. Georgia, Azerbaijan and Mongolia export too little of the listed goods to Russia for a comparison; for them, only the EU leg is shown. Aircraft (88) are not in the control: one aircraft sale can be $100–500M. Three route countries are too few to prove an EAEU effect; the table shows the difference, not its cause.

    Price per kilogram

    Each row is one code: its price per kg from the EU, from China, and to Russia, on one log scale. A price to Russia near the EU price fits EU-made goods; near the China price, Chinese or other cheaper goods.

    One customs code holds cheap and dear goods, so the price points to an origin but does not prove it. Hollow dots: the reporter estimated the weight. Uzbekistan estimates or leaves out most weights, so it has few or no rows. Codes under $100k a year to Russia are left out.

    Sanctions events, 2023–2025

    For each event: the average month in the 6 months after it against the 6 months before it (the event month is left out). Three questions: did the listed goods fall; did trade move to the other codes of the same HS4 headings; did it move to other countries.

    The comparison is the other two route countries for an event aimed at one country. For an event that applied to all three, it is EU exports to Türkiye, the UAE, Georgia and Azerbaijan. Below $50k a month before, the change is in dollars. Other things change in the same months, so a change after an event does not prove that the event caused it. Kazakhstan 2024: its statistics bureau; Comtrade has only January and February.

    Part 5 How much reached Russia

    How much reached Russia through each country

    Two measures, kept apart. Through the country: goods that physically passed through it. The low end is the country's own record of exports to Russia. The high end adds the shipments that reached the country but are missing from its import records, above its pre-war gap, on the EU leg and the China leg: if all of them went on to Russia, this is the ceiling. Sold by the country's companies: Russia's own customs records by country of seller, which include goods shipped from other countries.
