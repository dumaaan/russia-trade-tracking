The Central Asian detour

Battlefield goods are the 50 types of electronics, bearings and machine tools that Western governments found in Russian weapons in Ukraine. This page follows them on one route: exported by the EU to Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, then exported by those two countries to Russia.

Both legs, year by year

    Show the numbers

    Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan compared

    Kazakhstan's flows are six to ten times larger. Kyrgyzstan's grew much faster, from a very small base.

    The ratio is not a re-export share. The EU is not the only supplier: China and other countries also sell these goods to Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan. Kazakhstan's Tier 3.B exports to Russia (bearings, optics) are larger than the EU's Tier 3.B exports to Kazakhstan in every year.

    Show the numbers

    Does the EU shipment arrive?

    Each line divides what the country records as imports from the EU by what the EU records as exports to it. Below 100%, part of the shipments is missing from the country's records.

    Some gap is normal: goods arrive in another year, importers record the country of origin, and import values include freight. So each year is compared with the country's own pre-war ratio. The excess gap is the part above that level. Kazakhstan gives no product detail for imports by partner for 2020–2022, so its pre-war ratio uses 2019 only.
