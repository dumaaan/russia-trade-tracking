The Central Asian detour

Battlefield goods are the 50 types (customs codes) of electronics, bearings and machine tools that Western governments found in Russian weapons in Ukraine. The map follows them from the EU and from China through Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Armenia into Russia. The site uses 49 of the 50 codes: the code for light sensors has no comparable data before 2022.

    Part of the EU's shipments never appear in the importers' records

    Each line divides what the country records as imports from the EU by what the EU records as exports to it. Below 100%, part of the shipments is missing from the country's records.

    Some gap is normal: goods arrive in another year, importers record the country of origin, and import values include freight. So each year is compared with the country's own pre-war ratio. The excess gap is the part above that level. Kazakhstan gives no product detail for imports by partner for 2020–2022, so its pre-war ratio uses 2019 only.

    Listed goods as a share of all trade

    Each line tracks listed battlefield goods as a share of total trade value—on the left, EU exports to the country; on the right, the country's exports to Russia.

    A flat line means these goods are keeping pace with overall trade. A rising line means they are outpacing it. Each column uses its own scale.

    The April 2026 EU ban on Kyrgyzstan

    On 23 April 2026 the EU banned exports to Kyrgyzstan of machining centres (8457.10) and data transmission equipment (8517.62). Averages a month: April 2025 to March 2026 before the ban, May 2026 onwards after it. Each small chart is one row's months, on its own scale.
